Every tank water heater has a limited life, and most reach the end somewhere between year eight and year twelve. How long yours lasts in South Lyon comes down to water hardness, water pressure, and whether the tank was ever flushed or its anode rod replaced. The warning signs are familiar: hot water with a rusty tint, the pop and rumble of sediment, shorter and shorter showers, and, most urgently, water around the base of the tank. That last one settles the question. When the inner tank rusts through, the leak cannot be patched, and the only real fix is a new heater.

A new water heater should fit the way your household uses hot water today, not the way it did when the old one went in. That is why South Lyon Water Heater starts with a few questions: who showers when, whether there is a big soaking tub, and how many fixtures run at the same time. Tank heaters are compared by first-hour rating, while tankless units are sized by gallons per minute at the temperature rise needed in the coldest months. For a heat pump model, we check space and airflow, because it draws its heat from the surrounding room. We also confirm your gas line, vent, and electrical panel can handle the new unit, so there are no surprises on installation day.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. We drain and remove the old heater, set the new one level, and install fresh supply connectors and a working shutoff. On gas units, the connection gets a sediment trap and a leak test, and the vent is built to the manufacturer's spec. The relief valve discharge goes where it should, and we add an expansion tank, drain pan, or earthquake straps wherever local code requires them, including the permit if one applies. When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs

Early Action Saves Money

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact South Lyon Water Heater at (947) 227-7625 for a prompt inspection.

Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. A gas unit needs a leak-free gas connection with a sediment trap, a vent that is sized and pitched correctly so carbon monoxide goes outside, and combustion air it can actually reach. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Each tank needs a properly rated relief valve whose discharge pipe ends somewhere safe, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Moving to tankless or a heat pump raises the stakes further, with larger gas lines, new vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the new heater to your household, confirm the gas line, vent, and electrical service can support it, and install every code item your home requires. Before leaving, we leak-test, verify draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature. That is the difference between a heater that runs quietly for a decade and one that starts with a problem on day one. And if a repair would buy you several good years on your current tank, we tell you that honestly instead of selling you a new one.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
