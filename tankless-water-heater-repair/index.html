A tankless water heater keeps no reserve of hot water. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is a big part of its appeal, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In South Lyon, MI, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your South Lyon home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. Only then do we recommend a fix, and we can show you exactly why.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Repair?

Consider the risks first. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. On the electric side, the loads are large, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Scale is removed with a pump through the service valves rather than vinegar poured in and left to chance. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also helps you stay within the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
