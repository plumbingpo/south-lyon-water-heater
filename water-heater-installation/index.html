Every one of them involves new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Good installations start on paper. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. Gas units need a way to vent outdoors and enough air for combustion. A heat pump model needs space, a room that stays reasonably warm, and a condensate drain. An electric tankless unit may need a heavy dedicated circuit and enough panel capacity to support it. A heater in an attic or above finished space needs a drain pan piped to a safe spot. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. At the first hint of a problem, reach out to South Lyon Water Heater at (947) 227-7625 and we'll take a look.

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Installation

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. A vent outside the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, or clearance can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits that match their load, sometimes more than an older panel can supply. The plumbing side needs a correctly rated relief valve and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

A professional installation covers the whole job, not just setting the heater. Sizing by first-hour rating or flow rate, tested gas piping, a vent routed to spec, a circuit matched to the load, and the expansion tank, pan, or straps your code requires are all included. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. The result is an installation that is safe, sized correctly, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, you will hear it from us directly.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
