No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. South Lyon Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing on every job, handling everything from a burned-out heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

The trouble with water heater failures is that many of them start inside the tank where you can't see them. Minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod gets eaten away gradually, and when it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. If the water turns rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Preventive Care

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. Once a year, we flush the sediment out of the tank, measure the anode rod, test the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all tailored to the equipment in your home.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. An older tank needs a closer look than a brand-new one, a tankless unit on hard water needs regular descaling to keep its heat exchanger working, and a large family works the tank far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your equipment and water, so every service keeps the unit efficient and extends the life of the equipment you've already paid for.

Consider maintenance as protection for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers what you pay to heat water while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most families, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can hold when the incoming water is cold. That's why we never just copy the size of the old heater. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Choosing a new water heater is about more than the number of gallons. We'll compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, while a properly sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

The majority of repair calls we get come down to a handful of familiar problems. There's no hot water at all. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank rumbles and pops, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Add rusty water, sulfur smells, and dripping relief valves, and you have the full list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element just means lukewarm showers. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and a slow leak at the base can quietly ruin subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. Our technicians carry the parts to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
