A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. Most failures trace back to one of those parts. In South Lyon, MI, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across South Lyon, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Keep one rule in mind: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and South Lyon Water Heater can take over from there.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. We replace thermocouples, flame sensors, pilot assemblies, and igniters, clean burners and flame arrestor screens, swap failed gas control valves, and correct vent connectors that have separated or lost their slope. We also flush sediment, the cause of that popping or rumbling sound and a major reason tanks overheat at the bottom and fail early. When the tank itself is leaking or the heater is deep into its service life, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. New installations are sized by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and include the right venting, a sediment trap on the gas line, an expansion tank where needed, and seismic straps and a drain pan where code requires them. When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, because the biggest hazards are invisible. A fitting that is almost tight can seep gas for days before anyone notices. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically require a permit and an inspection. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

The value of calling a pro is certainty. We test instead of guessing, from thermocouple millivolt readings and burner flame checks to gas pressure at the valve and a draft test at the hood. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
