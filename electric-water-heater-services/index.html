An electric water heater has no burner, no flame, and no vent, so it is tempting to think nothing much can go wrong. In practice, the parts that do the work (two heating elements, two thermostats, and a high-limit safety switch) fail in predictable ways. The upper thermostat heats the top third of the tank first, then switches power to the lower element. Lose the upper element or thermostat and the lower element never gets power, so the whole tank goes cold. If the lower element burns out, the top of the tank still heats, so you get a short burst of hot water that runs out fast. For South Lyon, MI homeowners, knowing which symptom you have is the first step toward the right fix.

Resetting a tripped red button or swapping an element blindly rarely solves the problem for long. Our approach in South Lyon homes is to test the whole circuit. We confirm the breaker and the 240-volt supply, measure the resistance of both elements, check each element for a short to ground, and verify that each thermostat opens and closes as it should. The wiring gets inspected too, from the junction box to the thermostat terminals. A high-limit that keeps tripping almost always has a reason, such as a thermostat stuck closed or a grounded element, and we find it so the button stays set.

Most electric water heater repairs are finished in one visit. We replace burned-out elements (draining the tank first and refilling it completely before power goes back on, since an element energized in open air burns out in seconds), replace upper and lower thermostats and high-limit switches, repair scorched wiring and loose connections, and flush out sediment that is burying the lower element. When the tank is leaking or the heater has passed about twelve to fifteen years, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. New installations include confirming that the circuit, breaker, and wire size match the heater, plus an expansion tank, drain pan, and seismic straps wherever code calls for them. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A burning smell or a discolored access panel near the thermostat
  • A shower that turns lukewarm much sooner than it used to
  • Dangerously hot water, a sign a thermostat may be stuck
  • A tank that has stopped heating completely
  • A heater that works after a reset, then quits again
  • A hissing or sizzling noise near the lower element
  • The water heater breaker tripping over and over
  • Rust-colored hot water or moisture around the heater

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and operating cost
  • Protect your elements from hard water with a softener or upgraded low-watt-density elements
  • Drain a few gallons from the bottom of the tank periodically to control sediment
  • Get a diagnosis for any reset button that trips more than once
  • Ask for an anode rod check every few years, since the anode is what keeps the tank from rusting
  • Check the T&P relief valve annually, and call if it drips afterward
  • After any draining, refill the tank fully and run a hot faucet before restoring power
