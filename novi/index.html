Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Novi

Many early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Novi

Around Novi, water heater trouble tends to come from a handful of causes. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping you may hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod has often been eaten away, leaving the steel open to rust. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: thermocouples, burners, and venting on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well may see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater in hard water should get regular descaling. And an older installation being replaced may need updates it never had: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. All of these can be fixed, and most are much cheaper when caught early.
