Keeping Your Livonia Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Livonia

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them in Livonia. Gas tanks usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks lose heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
