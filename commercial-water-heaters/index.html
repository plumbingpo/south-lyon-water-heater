Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Rising gas or electric bills with no change in business volume
  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Fixtures that run too hot one minute and cool the next
  • Water temperatures that worry you before a health inspection
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush
  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • Equipment approaching the end of its expected service life

Why Hire a Professional for Commercial Water Heaters?

The engineering behind commercial hot water is more demanding. Large gas inputs require confirming the supply pressure, meter size, combustion air, and vent capacity. Commercial electric units may need dedicated high-amperage circuits. High storage temperatures, commonly 140 degrees or above for sanitation, depend on thermostatic mixing valves to bring delivery down to safe levels at hand sinks and showers. Recirculation adds pumps, timers, and check and balancing valves that need proper adjustment for hot water to reach every fixture on time.

Finally, commercial work runs on a deadline. Every hour without hot water is an hour of lost business, so the job has to be planned: permits pulled, inspections scheduled, gas and electrical capacity confirmed, and the right equipment on site before anything is disconnected. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Do not store supplies against the equipment or block air openings
  • Plan replacement before an aging unit fails during peak season
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Inspect anode rods regularly, since heavy use consumes them faster
  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
